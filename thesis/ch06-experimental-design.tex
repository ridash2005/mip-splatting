\label{ch:design}

The full protocol, verified against repository source, is the companion document

states the design; that document states the commands.

\section{Compute envelope and what it forbids}
Free tier: Kaggle $2\times$T4, 16\,GB each, compute capability 7.5. 3DGS states
that 24\,GB is required for paper-quality training, so numerical identity with
the published tables is unavailable for the largest scenes. Three acceptance
levels are declared in advance.

L3 is standard practice: 3DGS itself marks its Mip-NeRF 360 row as copied and
reports its own re-run separately ($27.58$ against the copied $27.69$). An
independent prior on implementation identity is the ${\sim}0.6$\,dB spread
between faithful splatting implementations on the same benchmark --- three times
the L1 tolerance.

\section{Reproduction, and the single-codebase decision}
\label{sec:onecodebase}
Both baselines run inside the Mip-Splatting codebase. The 3DGS arm is that
codebase with the 3D filter zeroed and \texttt{kernel\_size}~$=0.3$, which by
Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction} is 3DGS exactly.

This is forced as well as desirable. The multi-scale benchmark data is written
in mip-NeRF multiscale format (\texttt{metadata.json}), and only
Mip-Splatting's \texttt{sceneLoadTypeCallbacks} carries a \texttt{Multi-scale}
loader, so the original 3DGS repository cannot read it at all --- it falls
through every branch of the dispatch and dies. It also could not work in
principle: \texttt{readMultiScale} sets the field of view \emph{per frame},
whereas the Blender reader takes one global \texttt{camera\_angle\_x}.

The benefit is methodological: both arms share one dataloader, one metrics
implementation and one LPIPS backbone (VGG), so any difference between them is
the method rather than the plumbing. The diff that separates the arms is four
files, seven insertions and one deletion, and the build asserts that on every
run.

\section{The stress suite}
\label{sec:stress}
Equation~\eqref{eq:anisotropy} predicts that the method's advantage is a
function of parallax. Testing that requires captures of varying conditioning,
which are constructed as camera subsets of images already downloaded --- no new
data, no extra GPU time: full orbit (control, $O(1)$ conditioning, parity
expected by Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction}); one-sided arc
($\lambda_3/\lambda_1 \approx 0.03$); low-parallax cone ($\approx 0.003$, the
largest predicted gain); mixed focal (a random half downsampled $2$--$4\times$,
which tests the floor's mixed-camera defect of \S\ref{sec:defects}); and grazing
(high-incidence views only).

\section{Three instruments, attached to the baseline runs}
\label{sec:instruments}
All read-only with respect to the optimiser, so the reproduction remains a
reproduction. Together they test the method's premise before the method exists.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{The instruments and what each decides.}
  \label{tab:instruments}
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{lp{0.34\linewidth}p{0.42\linewidth}}
    \toprule
    & measurement & what it decides \\
    \midrule
    I-1 & distribution of \texttt{filter\_3D} at end of training, per scene, against scene scale and distance to nearest camera & whether $s^2/\lambda_i$ ever exceeds $f_k^2$. If the floor binds almost everywhere, Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction} applies universally, the method collapses to the baseline, and the project must pivot. The cheapest possible falsification, and a by-product of a run already scheduled. \\
    I-2 & $\lambda_3/\lambda_1$ per primitive on trained, unmodified checkpoints, per capture protocol; pure PyTorch, no CUDA & whether Equation~\eqref{eq:anisotropy}'s regime table holds on real scenes. Turns \S\ref{sec:anisotropy} from prediction into evidence before any method code is written. \\
    I-3 & per-scene mean and standard deviation across seeds, both baselines & the $\sigma$ that makes ``parity'' a quantity. Parity is then reported as within $k\sigma$ of the self-run baseline. \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

One implementation detail with a real failure mode:
\texttt{compute\_3D\_filter} assigns primitives that no camera sees
\texttt{distance[valid].max()}. These must be excluded or flagged in I-1's
histogram, or they form a spurious right tail that would be misread as the floor
binding --- the exact wrong answer at gate G3.

\section{Metrics, baselines, statistics}
Quality is PSNR, SSIM and LPIPS (VGG) per scene and averaged, at test scales
$1\times$, $\tfrac12$, $\tfrac14$, $\tfrac18$. Cost is primitive count, model
MB, peak training VRAM, wall-clock and render fps --- same GPU, same run.
Diagnostics are the fraction of primitives where the estimation term exceeds the
floor, and the mean anisotropy of $\Sigma_{\mathrm{filt}}$; this is what makes a
parity result interpretable rather than mysterious. Statistics are three seeds
minimum, mean $\pm$ standard deviation, paired per-scene deltas.

\paragraph{One measurement the shipped pipeline cannot make.}
\texttt{render.py} writes every test view into a single directory named by the
\texttt{-r} flag, not by the per-image scale, and \texttt{metrics.py} averages
that directory into one number. The shipped pipeline therefore produces one
figure where the paper reports four, and the per-scale table of
Chapter~\ref{ch:results} does not exist without recovering the mapping.
\texttt{tools/split\_by\_scale.py} recovers it from
\texttt{metadata.json} order, which \texttt{readMultiScale} preserves and
\texttt{render\_set} indexes by enumeration. Re-rendering at
\texttt{-r 1 2 4 8} would \emph{resample} the images and answer a different
question; it is not done. The recovery is validated by requiring the
count-weighted mean of the four groups to reproduce \texttt{metrics.py}'s single
pooled number, which is asserted on every scene of every run.
