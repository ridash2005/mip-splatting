\section{Where the band-limit lives}

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{The same quantity --- the finest structure the capture can determine
           --- estimated with successively more of the available geometry.}
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{p{0.2\linewidth}p{0.24\linewidth}p{0.26\linewidth}p{0.2\linewidth}}
    \toprule
    method & where the limit lives & how it is set & granularity \\
    \midrule
    NeRF (2020) & positional-encoding band $L$ & by hand, from ``the maximum frequency present in the sampled input images'' & one scalar, whole model \\
    3DGS (2023) & nowhere & not set --- no regularisation is applied & absent \\
    Mip-Splatting (2024) & variance added to the covariance & $f_k^2$ from nearest depth and largest focal, $s=0.2$ & one scalar per primitive \\
    \textbf{this work} & covariance added to the covariance, floored at the above & accumulated sampling Fisher information & per primitive, per direction \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The progression is not a sequence of unrelated tricks. NeRF used none of the
geometry beyond a global glance at the images; 3DGS used none at all;
Mip-Splatting used one view's worth; this work uses all of it.

\label{sec:cost}

  tested at four. 3DGS loses $15.64$\,dB in the published numbers as the test

  \label{fig:degradation}

The left panel is the experiment this project inherits, and its magnitude is the
reason the direction is worth pursuing: an $11$\,dB gap between a method with a
band-limit and a method without one says the band-limit is doing most of the
work. The question is then whether the \emph{right} band-limit does more.
Chapter~\ref{ch:results} reports this project's own measurement of the same
quantity, which is the control for everything that follows.

\section{The three specific defects of the scalar filter}
\label{sec:defects}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{It ignores parallax.} $f_k$ depends on one view. A primitive seen
        from fifty directions is assigned the same limit as one seen from a single
        direction at the same distance, although the former is far better
        localised.
  \item \textbf{It is isotropic.} The lateral limit is applied along depth, where
        a single view supplies no constraint at all.
        Section~\ref{sec:anisotropy} shows this under-smooths depth by a factor
        $1/\sin(\theta/2)$.
  \item \textbf{It mixes cameras.} $d_{\min}$ and $f_{\max}$ come from different
        views, so the quotient corresponds to no physical pixel footprint. The
        repository itself carries the comment
        \texttt{\#TODO consider focal length and image width} on the current
        branch.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Concurrent work, and what it leaves for this thesis}
\label{sec:relatedwork}

A due-diligence review was run before the stress suite, with one question: has
anyone already made the \emph{3D} filter anisotropic or per-direction? The answer
is partly yes, and the contribution stated in \S\ref{ch:intro} has been narrowed
accordingly rather than defended. Two of the five papers below change what this
thesis may claim; the change is recorded here, in the chapter that sets up the
gap, and not buried in the discussion.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{The five papers that could have pre-empted this work, and the line of
  separation in each case. The review is recorded in full in
  \texttt{docs/C12-related-work.md}, three lines per paper: the claim, the
  mechanism, and the evidence that settled it.}
  \label{tab:related}
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{p{0.21\linewidth}p{0.34\linewidth}p{0.37\linewidth}}
    \toprule
    work & what it does & relation to this thesis \\
    \midrule
    \textbf{AAA-Gaussians} (arXiv:2504.12811, 2025)
      & Replaces the 2D Mip filter with a full $3\times3$ 3D filter, whose
        anisotropy is decomposed about the \emph{current view ray} and
        recomputed every rendered frame.
      & \textbf{Removes the novelty of ``an anisotropic 3D filter''.} What
        remains is the \emph{source} of the anisotropy: theirs is the view being
        rendered, this thesis's is the conditioning of the capture that was
        available. The two are composable, not competing. \\
    \addlinespace[2pt]
    \textbf{PUP 3D-GS} (CVPR 2025)
      & A second-order sensitivity of the photometric error in each primitive's
        spatial parameters, accumulated over training views, collapsed to a
        scalar and thresholded to prune $90\,\%$ of primitives.
      & \textbf{The matrix itself is not new.} It is built from the same
        ingredients as $\Lambda_k$. The divergence is what survives: PUP keeps a
        scalar and discards the eigenstructure, which is precisely what B1 uses. \\
    \addlinespace[2pt]
    \textbf{FisherRF} (ECCV 2024)
      & Fisher information over model parameters as an acquisition function for
        next-best-view selection.
      & Same statistical object, opposite direction of use: it chooses which
        camera to \emph{acquire}; B1 takes the cameras as given. Changes the
        dataset, not the primitive. \\
    \addlinespace[2pt]
    \textbf{3DGS-MCMC} (NeurIPS 2024)
      & Recasts densify/prune/reset as MCMC sampling, removing the heuristics and
        the dependence on initialisation.
      & Orthogonal and composable. No filter, no band-limit, no covariance floor.
        Cited as the nearest principled density-control work. \\
    \addlinespace[2pt]
    \textbf{Analytic-Splatting} (ECCV 2024)
      & Integrates the Gaussian response over the square pixel window in closed
        form instead of approximating the window by a 2D Gaussian.
      & Improves the \emph{2D} half of Mip-Splatting; this thesis changes the
        \emph{3D} half. Directly composable. \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\keybox{what the claim is, after the narrowing}{%
Not ``an anisotropic 3D filter'' --- AAA-Gaussians has one. Not ``a per-primitive
second-order information matrix'' --- PUP 3D-GS builds one. The claim that
survives, and the only one the experiments are asked to support, is a
\emph{per-primitive band-limit read off the conditioning of the training
capture}: a quantity fixed once per primitive by the cameras that saw it, rather
than by the camera now looking at it, and retaining the spectrum rather than a
scalar summary of it. A primitive seen from a narrow baseline is
under-determined along depth no matter which view renders it, and a filter
derived from the render-time ray carries no memory of that. The \texttt{cone} and
\texttt{arc} protocols of \S\ref{sec:stress} exist to isolate exactly that
regime, and nobody has tested it, because nobody else's filter is a function of
the training-camera spread.}
