3D Gaussian Splatting reconstructs a radiance field from a finite, non-uniform
set of camera samples, but places no limit on how small a primitive may become.
Mip-Splatting supplies one: a scalar variance floor per primitive,
$f_k^2 = 0.2\,(d_{\min}/f_{\max})^2$, added in quadrature to all three axes.
This work argues that the correct limit is neither scalar nor isotropic.
Treating primitive position as a parameter estimated from noisy image
measurements, the accumulated sampling Fisher information
\[
  \Lambda_k \;=\; \sum_n w_{nk}\,(J_nW_n)^{\!\top}\,\Sigma_{\mathrm{pix}}^{-1}\,(J_nW_n)
\]
--- a per-primitive observability matrix, and the Fisher information of that
measurement model at unit weights --- gives a direction-dependent floor on how
precisely the capture localises that primitive. Cram\'er--Rao motivates the form;
the claim tested is the weaker one that structure finer than $1/\sqrt{\lambda_i}$
along eigendirection $i$ is not identifiable from these images.

We prove three things. First, Mip-Splatting's filter is exactly the
single-view, lateral-only, isotropised special case of this expression, and its
constant $\sqrt{0.2}$ is precisely the product $s\,\sigma_{\mathrm{pix}}$ --- a
confidence radius of $0.447$ pixels, which identifies a magic number in a
published paper. Second, the anisotropy has a closed form in three regimes: a
single view is already anisotropic off-axis by $\cos^2\varphi$; two views
subtending $\theta$ give the triaxial spectrum
$\{2,\,2\cos^2(\theta/2),\,2\sin^2(\theta/2)\}$; and a \emph{distribution} of
views over an angular span --- which is what a capture protocol actually is ---
obeys a cap form that the two-view expression understates by $\sqrt{2}$. Against
that form, the measured conditioning of five capture protocols agrees to
$10$--$15\,\%$, where the two-view expression is low by $60\,\%$. Third,
flooring the anisotropic filter at Mip-Splatting's own value makes the method
reduce to that baseline exactly when the floor dominates, so parity on
well-conditioned benchmarks is a structural guarantee rather than an empirical
hope. We additionally propose a per-primitive angular identifiability criterion
that masks spherical-harmonic coefficients the capture cannot determine ---
76\,\% of the model's parameters.

Concurrent work has narrowed what may be claimed here, and the narrowing is
stated rather than defended. AAA-Gaussians (2025) already makes the 3D filter a
full $3\times3$, derived from the view being rendered; PUP 3D-GS (2025) already
builds a per-primitive second-order spatial sensitivity, which it collapses to a
scalar and thresholds for pruning. What is new is therefore neither the
anisotropy nor the matrix, but the \emph{source} of the anisotropy --- the
conditioning of the training capture, fixed once per primitive --- and the
retention of its \emph{spectrum}.
