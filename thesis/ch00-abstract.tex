3D Gaussian Splatting builds a radiance field out of several hundred thousand
small translucent ellipsoids, fitted by gradient descent to a set of
photographs. Nothing in that procedure sets a lower bound on how small a
primitive may become. Mip-Splatting supplies one: a scalar variance floor,
$f_k^2 = 0.2\,(d_{\min}/f_{\max})^2$, added in quadrature to all three axes of
every primitive. This thesis argues that the correct bound is neither scalar nor
isotropic.

The reasoning treats a primitive's position as something the photographs
measure. Each camera that sees it contributes one noisy observation, and the
precision of that observation is direction-dependent: a camera pins a point down
well across its line of sight and poorly along it. Accumulating over all the
cameras that saw the primitive gives a $3\times3$ matrix,
\[
  \Lambda_k \;=\; \sum_n w_{nk}\,(J_nW_n)^{\!\top}\,\Sigma_{\mathrm{pix}}^{-1}\,(J_nW_n),
\]
the Fisher information of that measurement model at unit weights. Its
eigenvectors are the directions the capture constrained well and badly, and its
eigenvalues say by how much. The claim tested is that structure finer than
$1/\sqrt{\lambda_i}$ along eigendirection $i$ is not identifiable from these
images.

Three results follow. First, Mip-Splatting's filter is a special case of this
expression, obtained by keeping one view, discarding the depth direction and
averaging what remains; its constant $\sqrt{0.2}$, introduced without
derivation, is exactly $s\,\sigma_{\mathrm{pix}}$, a confidence radius of $0.447$
pixels. Second, the anisotropy has a closed form in three regimes: within a
single view it falls off as $\cos^2\varphi$ away from the optical axis; across
two views separated by $\theta$ the spectrum is triaxial,
$\{2,\,2\cos^2(\theta/2),\,2\sin^2(\theta/2)\}$; and across a distribution of
views over an angular window, which is what a capture protocol is, the correct
expression is a spherical-cap form that the two-view result understates by
$\sqrt{2}$. Measured at each protocol's own width, the observed conditioning of
the $\capAgreeN$ parallax protocols agrees with the cap form to
$\capAgreeMin$--$\capAgreeMax\,\%$, where the two-view expression falls short by
up to $\twoViewLowMax\,\%$. Third, flooring the anisotropic limit at
Mip-Splatting's own value makes the two methods coincide exactly wherever that
value dominates, so parity on a well-conditioned benchmark is structural and an
improvement there would indicate a bug.

A second proposal applies the same reasoning on the sphere of directions,
masking the spherical-harmonic coefficients a capture cannot determine. Those
are $76\,\%$ of the model's parameters. Chapter~\ref{ch:results} reports the
controlled test of it.

Concurrent work narrows what can be claimed for the main idea, and the narrowing
is stated here. AAA-Gaussians (2025) already makes the 3D filter a full
$3\times3$, though derived from the view being rendered. PUP 3D-GS (2025)
already builds a per-primitive second-order spatial sensitivity, though it
collapses that to a scalar and uses it to prune. What remains new is where the
anisotropy comes from: the conditioning of the training capture, fixed once per
primitive, with its whole spectrum kept.
