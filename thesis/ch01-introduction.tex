\chapter{Introduction --- the question, and why it is a signal-processing question}
\label{ch:intro}

A radiance field is reconstructed from a finite set of photographs. The
photographs sample the scene non-uniformly: closer surfaces are sampled more
densely than distant ones, some regions are seen from many directions and others
from a narrow cone, and different cameras contribute different pixel footprints.
Any reconstruction is therefore subject to a limit on resolvable detail, and
that limit is a property of the capture, not of the representation chosen to
hold it.

The originators of NeRF understood this and said so. Their positional encoding
uses $L$ frequency bands, and they set $L$ from, in their words, ``the maximum
frequency present in the sampled input images''. That is the right instinct
applied with the bluntest possible instrument: one scalar, chosen by hand, for
an entire scene.

3D Gaussian Splatting removed even that. Its authors state plainly that no
regularisation is applied. A primitive may shrink without bound; nothing in the
objective prevents it. The consequence is visible the moment the test sampling
rate departs from the training one --- Section~\ref{sec:cost} quantifies it, in
the published numbers at $15.64$\,dB and in our own reproduction at
\StmtArmBDrop\,dB.

Mip-Splatting restored a limit, and the restoration works. But the limit it
restores is a single scalar per primitive, added in quadrature to all three axes
of the covariance. This thesis asks what that scalar should have been, and
answers that it should not have been a scalar at all.

\section{The claim}

\keybox{thesis}{%
``The maximum frequency present in the sampled input images'' is not one number.
It falls with depth; it differs across the ray from along it; and it varies over
the sphere of directions. The accumulated sampling Fisher information is its
local, directional generalisation. Floored at the per-view Nyquist limit, it
reduces to Mip-Splatting exactly where the capture is well conditioned, and
departs from it precisely where the capture is not.}

\section{Why this belongs in an Electrical Engineering department}

Stripped of its graphics vocabulary, the problem is reconstruction from
non-uniform samples without a matched prefilter --- a classical
signal-processing failure mode. The objects involved are familiar ones.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{The same objects, in two vocabularies.}
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{p{0.42\linewidth}p{0.5\linewidth}}
    \toprule

    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}

The contribution is to compute the acquisition-side prefilter from estimation
theory rather than choosing it, and to make it anisotropic because the
information is.

\section{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item A derivation of the per-primitive, per-direction band-limit from the
        Fisher information of the capture geometry, using the same Jacobian the
        renderer already forms (\S\ref{sec:jacobian}--\S\ref{sec:accumulation}).
  \item A proof that Mip-Splatting's scalar filter is the single-view,
        lateral-only, isotropised special case, with its constant $\sqrt{0.2}$
        identified as $s\,\sigma_{\mathrm{pix}} = 0.447$\,px
        (\S\ref{sec:proposition1}).
  \item An exact expression for the anisotropy,
        $\sigma_{\mathrm{depth}}/\sigma_{\mathrm{lat}} = 1/\sin(\theta/2)$, which
        predicts where the method must help and where it must not
        (\S\ref{sec:anisotropy}).
  \item An exact-reduction theorem making parity on well-conditioned benchmarks
        structural (\S\ref{sec:proposition2}).
  \item An angular identifiability criterion for spherical-harmonic
        coefficients --- 76\,\% of the model (Chapter~\ref{ch:method2}).
  \item A verified, executed reproduction of both baselines on free compute,
        with the twelve pre-flight assertions and the per-scale recovery that
        the shipped pipeline cannot produce (Chapters~\ref{ch:design} and
        \ref{ch:results}).
\end{enumerate}
