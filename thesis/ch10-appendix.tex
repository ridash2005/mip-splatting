\chapter{Derivations}

\section{Rank of a single view}
\label{app:rank}
For a pinhole camera of focal length $f$, a primitive at camera-frame
coordinates $(0,0,z)$ projects with Jacobian
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial(u,v)}{\partial(x,y,z)} =
  \begin{bmatrix} f/z & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & f/z & 0 \end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
so $I_n = \mathrm{diag}\big((f/z)^2, (f/z)^2, 0\big)/\sigma_{\mathrm{pix}}^2$ in
the camera frame. The third eigenvalue is exactly zero: a single view carries no
depth information. The lateral scale $s\,\sigma_{\mathrm{pix}}z/f$ of
\S\ref{sec:proposition1} follows immediately from the first two.

\section{Two symmetric views --- the exact spectrum}
\label{app:twoview}
Place two cameras at distance $z$, symmetric about the primitive, subtending
parallax angle $\theta$, with unit ray directions $d_1, d_2$. Each view
contributes $(f/z)^2(I - d_nd_n^{\!\top})/\sigma_{\mathrm{pix}}^2$ on-axis
(\S\ref{app:rank}), so up to the common factor
\begin{equation}
  \Lambda \;\propto\; 2I - d_1d_1^{\!\top} - d_2d_2^{\!\top}.
\end{equation}
Take coordinates with $\hat x$ the bisector of the two rays, $\hat y$ in their
plane and $\hat z$ perpendicular to it, so
$d_{1,2} = (\cos\tfrac\theta2,\ \pm\sin\tfrac\theta2,\ 0)$. Then
$d_1d_1^{\!\top} + d_2d_2^{\!\top} = \mathrm{diag}(2\cos^2\tfrac\theta2,\
2\sin^2\tfrac\theta2,\ 0)$ --- the off-diagonal terms cancel by symmetry --- and
$\Lambda$ is diagonal with
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{spec}(\Lambda) \;=\;
  \big\{\,\underbrace{2}_{\perp\text{ to the plane}},\ \
  \underbrace{2\cos^2(\theta/2)}_{\text{in-plane lateral}},\ \
  \underbrace{2\sin^2(\theta/2)}_{\text{along the bisector (depth)}}\,\big\}.
  \label{eq:twoviewspectrum}
\end{equation}
Two lines, and stronger than the descriptive argument it replaces.

\paragraph{The filter is triaxial, not merely elongated.} All three values differ
at a generic $\theta$. The in-plane lateral direction carries
$2\cos^2(\theta/2)$, strictly less than the $2$ available perpendicular to the
plane, so a two-view capture degrades \emph{two} directions and not only depth.
An isotropic filter is wrong along both; a filter elongated only along depth
would be wrong along one. This is why $\Sigma_{\mathrm{filt}}$ is built from the
full eigendecomposition of $\Lambda_k$ rather than from a depth axis and a
lateral scalar.

\paragraph{The ordering caveat.} $\lambda_3/\lambda_1 = \sin^2(\theta/2)$ is the
ratio of \emph{smallest} to \emph{largest} only while
$\sin^2(\theta/2) \le \cos^2(\theta/2)$, that is for $\theta \le 90^\circ$. Past
that the depth direction is better constrained than the in-plane lateral one and
the two exchange roles, so in general
$\lambda_{\min}/\lambda_{\max} = \min(\sin^2\tfrac\theta2, \cos^2\tfrac\theta2)$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:anisotropy} is stated with the restriction for this reason;
Table~\ref{tab:twoviewspectrum} shows the two forms diverging by a factor of
three at $\theta = 120^\circ$.

\section{A distribution of views}
\label{app:cap}
Equation~\eqref{eq:twoviewspectrum} is exact for two cameras and is applied in
this thesis only to two cameras. For a protocol --- ten or a hundred cameras over
an angular window --- $\Lambda$ is the sum over the distribution. For directions
uniform on a cap of half-angle $\alpha$ about the mean viewing direction,
$\cos t$ is uniform on $[\cos\alpha, 1]$, so with $c = \cos\alpha$,
\begin{equation}
  E \;\equiv\; \mathbb{E}[d_z^2] \;=\; \frac{1+c+c^2}{3},
  \qquad
  \mathbb{E}[d_x^2] = \mathbb{E}[d_y^2] = \frac{1-E}{2},
\end{equation}
and $\Lambda = N\big(I - \mathbb{E}[dd^{\!\top}]\big)$ is diagonal with
$\lambda_{\mathrm{depth}} = N(1-E)$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{lat}} = N(1+E)/2$,
giving Equation~\eqref{eq:cap}. Expanding at small $\alpha$: $E = 1 -
\alpha^2/2 + O(\alpha^4)$, so $\lambda_3/\lambda_1 \to \alpha^2/2$ against the
two-view $\sin^2\alpha \to \alpha^2$ --- a factor of exactly two, confirmed
numerically at $0.500013$ for a $1^\circ$ span. The closed form agrees with
$2\times10^5$ sampled directions per span to $3\times10^{-3}$ relative
(Table~\ref{tab:cap}).

\section{The opacity compensation carries over unchanged}
\label{app:mass}
\begin{lemma}[Mass preservation]
\label{lem:mass}
For any positive-definite $\Sigma$ and $\Sigma_{\mathrm{filt}}$, the update
$\alpha \leftarrow \alpha\sqrt{|\Sigma| / |\Sigma + \Sigma_{\mathrm{filt}}|}$
leaves $\int \alpha \exp\!\big(-\tfrac12 x^{\!\top}\Sigma^{-1}x\big)\,dx$
unchanged.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The integral is $\alpha(2\pi)^{3/2}\sqrt{|\Sigma|}$. Replacing $\Sigma$ by
$\Sigma + \Sigma_{\mathrm{filt}}$ multiplies it by
$\sqrt{|\Sigma + \Sigma_{\mathrm{filt}}|/|\Sigma|}$, which the update cancels
exactly. Nothing in the argument uses the form of $\Sigma_{\mathrm{filt}}$
beyond positive-definiteness.
\end{proof}
So Mip-Splatting's compensation needs no modification for the anisotropic case:
it is not a scalar identity that happens to hold, it is the general one they
instantiated at $\Sigma_{\mathrm{filt}} = f_k^2 I$. Verified to
$4\times10^{-16}$ relative over 500 random anisotropic $\Sigma_{\mathrm{filt}}$,
and to $6\times10^{-17}$ against Mip-Splatting's own scalar expression in the
isotropic case, by \texttt{tools/test\_geometry\_claims.py}.


\section{Proposition~\ref{prop:constant}, in full}
\label{app:prop1}
From \S\ref{app:rank}, the single-view lateral standard deviation at confidence
$s$ is $s\,\sigma_{\mathrm{pix}}z/f$. Mip-Splatting sets
$f_k = \sqrt{0.2}\,d_{\min}/f_{\max}$. Setting $z = d_{\min}$ and $f = f_{\max}$
and equating,
\begin{equation}
  s\,\sigma_{\mathrm{pix}}\,\frac{d_{\min}}{f_{\max}}
  = \sqrt{0.2}\,\frac{d_{\min}}{f_{\max}}
  \;\Longrightarrow\;
  s\,\sigma_{\mathrm{pix}} = \sqrt{0.2} = 0.4472\ \text{px}.
\end{equation}
The identification holds for every $d_{\min}$ and $f_{\max}$, so it is an
identity between the two formulations rather than a coincidence at one operating
point. Note that because Mip-Splatting takes $d_{\min}$ and $f_{\max}$ from
different cameras, the substitution above is the most favourable reading of their
formula; the proposed method uses each view's own $(d,f)$ pair and so is also
correctly coupled.
