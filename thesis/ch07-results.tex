Two of these deserve comment.

\paragraph{Check 4.1 --- what actually makes arm B 3DGS.} The construction of
\S\ref{sec:onecodebase} is an argument about source code, and an argument is not
a measurement. \texttt{render.py} never recomputes the 3D filter: it reads
\texttt{filter\_3D} back from the saved point cloud, which
\texttt{save\_ply} writes as a per-primitive property. The saved model is
therefore the artefact that decides whether arm B is really 3DGS. Read back
directly from the point clouds, $\max|\texttt{filter\_3D}|$ is
$\armBFilterMax$ for arm B and $\armAFilterMax$ for arm A. The reduction is
exact, not approximate.

\paragraph{Check 9 --- the per-scale mapping.} The count-weighted mean of the
four recovered scale groups reproduces \texttt{metrics.py}'s pooled PSNR to
within $0.01$\,dB on every scene of every run. Without this the four-column
table would be an assumption about directory ordering rather than a
measurement.

\subsection*{Gate G2, and why it does not pass}
\label{sec:g2}

G2 was declared in advance as: 3DGS falls $\gtrsim13$\,dB from full to
$\tfrac18$; Mip-Splatting falls $\lesssim6$\,dB; the gap at $\tfrac18$ exceeds
$9$\,dB. Measured on this run:

    \bottomrule

The protocol's instruction on a G2 failure is to stop, and to diagnose in a
stated order: (a) the splitter's index mapping; (b) whether arm B's
\texttt{filter\_3D} is really zero at step 30\,000; (c) \texttt{--white\_background}.
All three were checked, and none of them is the cause:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item The splitter's count-weighted mean reproduces \texttt{metrics.py}'s
        pooled PSNR to within $0.01$\,dB on every scene of every run --- the
        mapping is right, and it is asserted per scene rather than spot-checked.
  \item \texttt{filter\_3D} read back from all eight of arm B's saved point
        clouds is identically zero, and non-zero on all eight of arm A's. The
        reduction is exact.
  \item \texttt{--white\_background} was passed to every job.
\end{enumerate}

The cause is the one identified in \S\ref{sec:densification}: arm B is not
published 3DGS, because this repository's densification is GOF's. A better
densification recovers most of what the missing band-limit costs, so the
collapse that G2's thresholds were calibrated against does not occur here.
Arm B's $\tfrac18$ figure is $6.82$\,dB above published --- almost exactly the
$6.8$\,dB by which the measured gap falls short of the expected one.

\keybox{what this means, stated precisely}{%
G2 as written cannot be met in this codebase, and that is a property of the
gate's premise rather than of the experiment. The gate assumed the 3DGS arm
would carry published-3DGS densification; the single-codebase decision of
\S\ref{sec:onecodebase} --- which is \emph{forced} by the multi-scale loader, not
chosen for convenience --- brings GOF's densification with it, to both arms
equally.

What does reproduce, and is what the thesis actually relies on: 3DGS degrades
$1.9\times$ faster than Mip-Splatting across the same range ($9.09$ against
$4.86$\,dB), and Mip-Splatting leads at every reduced scale by a margin that
grows monotonically with the scale gap. That is correct in sign, ordering and
order of magnitude under a stated deviation, which is L2 by
Table~\ref{tab:levels}. The gate should be restated against a self-run control
rather than against a published number produced by a different densification,
and \S\ref{sec:densification} records the alternative that was considered and
not taken.}

\section{Why arm B sits above published 3DGS}
\label{sec:densification}

Arm A lands within half a decibel of the published Mip-Splatting numbers at
every scale of both protocols. Arm B does not: it is several decibels
\emph{above} published 3DGS, and by the largest margin at $\tfrac18$, where the
published method collapses hardest. A reproduction that overshoots its target by
five decibels is not a success, and it is not noise --- the run-to-run spread of
\S\ref{sec:spread} is smaller by two orders of magnitude. It has a cause, and
the cause matters for how both tables are read.

\keybox{the repository's densification is not 3DGS's}{%
\texttt{densify\_and\_prune} in this codebase splits on a quantile over
accumulated \emph{absolute} gradients:
\texttt{grads\_abs} is taken from \texttt{viewspace\_point\_tensor.grad[:,\,2:]}
and thresholded at $Q = \mathrm{quantile}(\texttt{grads\_abs},\,1-\rho)$, where
$\rho$ is the fraction of primitives passing the ordinary gradient test. That is
the Gaussian Opacity Fields criterion, added to \texttt{mip-splatting} in commit
\texttt{24af724} (April 2024) --- \emph{after} the Mip-Splatting paper's numbers
were produced. Original 3DGS splits on $\lVert\nabla\rVert \geq \tau_{\mathrm{pos}}$
alone.}

Arm B is therefore 3DGS's \emph{band-limit} semantics --- \texttt{filter\_3D}
identically zero and \texttt{kernel\_size}~$=0.3$, exact by
Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction} and verified on the saved models --- running on
GOF's \emph{densification}. It is exactly 3DGS in the sense this thesis needs,
and it is not the 3DGS whose numbers Mip-Splatting published.

This was anticipated. The reading list of Appendix~\ref{app:reading} carries the
GOF entry with the note ``a reproduction hazard if the commit is not pinned''.
It is now observed rather than predicted, and three consequences follow.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{Arm B carries no L1 claim against published 3DGS.} The published
        column in Tables~\ref{tab:stmt} and \ref{tab:mtmt} is a target for arm A
        and a \emph{reference point of a different configuration} for arm B. Its
        deltas are reported, and they are not reproduction error.
  \item \textbf{The between-arm comparison is unaffected.} Both arms share the
        densification, the dataloader, the metrics implementation and the LPIPS
        backbone; the four-file diff of \S\ref{sec:onecodebase} is the only
        difference between them. So the gap column remains exactly what
        \S\ref{sec:onecodebase} claims it is --- the method, not the plumbing ---
        and it is the control for all later work, which is the L3 discipline of
        Table~\ref{tab:levels}.
  \item \textbf{The published $\tfrac18$ gap is not reproducible in this
        codebase, and should not be quoted as if it were.} A better
        densification recovers much of what the missing band-limit costs. That
        is itself informative: it says the $15.64$\,dB figure of
        \S\ref{sec:cost} is a property of 3DGS-as-published, not a bound on
        what a filter-free method can do.
\end{enumerate}

Pinning the pre-GOF commit and re-running arm B would restore the published
comparison, at the cost of a densification that no longer matches arm A --- which
would move the difference between the arms out of the filter and back into the
plumbing. Given that Table~\ref{tab:levels} makes the self-run baseline the
control, the shared-densification choice is the correct one and the overshoot is
the price of it. The alternative is recorded here so the choice is visible.

\section{Run-to-run spread (I-3)}
\label{sec:spread}
Parity is not a testable claim without a $\sigma$. Repeats of an identical
configuration bound the harness's own nondeterminism --- CUDA atomics in the
rasteriser accumulate in a non-deterministic order, so bit-identical inputs do
not give bit-identical outputs.

\input{generated/table-seedspread}

The largest spread observed between repeats of one configuration is
$\repeatSpread$\,dB. Read against L1's $\pm0.20$\,dB tolerance, this says the
harness's own noise floor cannot by itself decide an L1 claim either way, which
is the precondition for using these baselines as a control.

\section{The instruments --- I-1 and I-2}
\label{sec:instrumentresults}

Both instruments are functions of the primitive positions and the capture
geometry alone, so neither needs the images, the rasteriser, or a training run
of its own. Both were therefore run over every trained scene, and over the five
capture protocols of \S\ref{sec:stress}, at a total cost of two minutes.

\input{generated/table-instruments}

\subsection*{I-1 on the standard benchmark: the floor binds everywhere}
On the full orbit, the estimation term $s^2/\lambda_i$ exceeds
Mip-Splatting's floor $f_k^2$ on \textbf{zero} primitives, in \emph{every}
direction of \emph{every} scene. The median ratio
$\sqrt{s^2/\lambda_{\min}}\,/\,f_k$ is $0.35$, and even at the 99th percentile
it is below $0.5$: the floor dominates the estimation term by a factor of two to
three throughout. No primitive is unseen by every camera, so the artefactual
right tail of \S\ref{sec:instruments} does not arise and nothing had to be
excluded.

By Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction} this means B1 \emph{is} Mip-Splatting on
these scenes --- exactly, not approximately. That is worth stating plainly:

\keybox{no gain is available on the standard Blender benchmark}{%
The eight Blender scenes are 100-camera full orbits, the best-conditioned
capture in the suite. Section~\ref{sec:anisotropy} predicts parity there, and
the measurement is stronger than the prediction: the anisotropy is $1.14\times$
where Table~\ref{tab:regimes} anticipated $1.6\times$. A method evaluated only
on this benchmark could not show an improvement, and if one appeared it would be
a bug.}

\subsection*{I-2 across the stress suite: the regime structure is real}
The refutation condition at gate G3 is that the floor exceeds the estimation
term on nearly every primitive in \emph{every} scene --- which would make
Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction} apply universally and collapse the method to
its baseline everywhere. It does not. Degrading the capture moves the balance
exactly as Equation~\eqref{eq:anisotropy} requires:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{One-sided arc} ($80^\circ$ span): the estimation term exceeds
        the floor along the worst-constrained direction on $100\,\%$ of
        primitives, while still lying below it in the best-constrained
        directions. That is precisely the anisotropic regime the filter is built
        for --- a scalar floor is wrong here in one direction and right in the
        others.
  \item \textbf{Low-parallax cone} ($20^\circ$ span): the estimation term
        exceeds the floor in \emph{all} directions on $100\,\%$ of primitives,
        by a median factor of $12$. Per scene the measured
        $\sigma_{\mathrm{depth}}/\sigma_{\mathrm{lat}}$ runs from $4.5\times$ at
        a $29^\circ$ span to $19.2\times$ at $10^\circ$ --- against the
        $19\times$ that Table~\ref{tab:regimes} predicts at $6^\circ$.
  \item \textbf{Grazing} views degrade the conditioning mildly ($1.45\times$),
        and \textbf{mixed focal} barely at all ($1.16\times$ against the full
        orbit's $1.14\times$).
\end{itemize}

So gates G3 and G4 are both passed, and the regime table of
\S\ref{sec:anisotropy} is confirmed as a measurement rather than left as a
prediction --- before a line of method code exists. This is the cheapest
falsification the project had available, and it did not fire.

\paragraph{One prediction not borne out.} Section~\ref{sec:defects} argues that
mixing cameras is the third defect of the scalar filter, and \S\ref{sec:stress}
expected the mixed-focal protocol to expose it. It does not show up in the
anisotropy: $1.16\times$ against $1.14\times$ is nothing. It does show up in the
magnitude --- the median ratio of estimation term to floor rises from $0.35$ to
$0.47$, a $35\,\%$ relative shift --- but that is still far below unity, so the
floor continues to bind and Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction} still applies. The
honest reading is that the mixed-camera defect is a statement about the
\emph{value} of $f_k$, not about its isotropy, and that this protocol as
constructed does not stress it hard enough to matter. Either the protocol needs
a wider focal spread than $2$--$4\times$, or the defect is real but small; the
present measurement does not distinguish those, and it is not reported as though
it did.

\section{What these results do and do not establish}
They establish a control. Both baselines now exist as this project's own
measurements, on known hardware, from pinned commits, with the arm separation
verified on the saved models rather than argued from source. Every later
comparison is against these numbers and not against the published ones, which is
the L3 discipline of Table~\ref{tab:levels}.

They establish nothing about the proposed method, because it has not been run.
The predictions in Tables~\ref{tab:stress} and \ref{tab:b2} were recorded before
any measurement and are left standing, unmarked and unhedged, so that the
comparison when it comes is not retrospective.
